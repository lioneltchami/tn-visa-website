# Part 1: Step-by-Step Application Walkthrough

## 1.1 The Decision Tree: Which Application Method Is Right for You?

There are three ways to obtain TN status. Your choice depends on your nationality, where you currently are, and your risk tolerance.

### Method A: Port of Entry (POE) — Canadian Citizens Only

**Best for:** First-time applicants and renewals who want same-day decisions

You physically go to a US border crossing or Canadian pre-clearance airport, present your documents to a CBP officer, and get a decision on the spot. If approved, you walk away with a new I-94 and can start working immediately.

**Pros:**
- Same-day decision (often within 1–3 hours)
- Lowest cost ($56 total: $50 application + $6 I-94 fee)
- No advance filing required
- Can apply any day the port is open

**Cons:**
- Must physically travel to the border
- If denied, you're outside the US with no pending application
- Officer discretion varies by port and day
- Cannot apply from inside the US

**Cost:** ~$56 USD (~$77 CAD)

### Method B: Form I-129 Petition (USCIS) — Both Canadians and Mexicans

**Best for:** People already in the US on another status (F-1, B-2, H-1B, etc.) who want to change to TN without leaving

Your employer files Form I-129 (Petition for a Nonimmigrant Worker) with USCIS. You stay in the US while it processes.

**Pros:**
- No need to leave the US
- 240-day automatic work authorization extension if filed before current status expires
- More predictable than border officer discretion
- Paper trail provides documentation

**Cons:**
- Standard processing: 3–7 months (FY2026 average ~4.2 months)
- Premium processing available but expensive ($2,965 as of March 2026)
- Base filing fee: $460–$1,015 depending on employer size
- Additional Asylum Program Fee: $300–$600
- Cannot travel internationally while pending (petition considered abandoned)

**Cost:** $810–$4,420+ USD depending on employer size and premium processing

### Method C: Consular Application — Mexican Citizens (Required)

**Best for:** Mexican citizens applying for the first time or renewing with travel

Mexican citizens must obtain a TN visa stamp at a US embassy or consulate before entering the US. There is no border shortcut for Mexican nationals.

**Pros:**
- Required process — no alternative for initial Mexican TN applications
- Visa stamp valid for 1–4 years depending on reciprocity fee paid
- Can schedule appointment in advance

**Cons:**
- Requires DS-160 completion and consular interview
- Interview wait times: 1–4 weeks (as of April 2026)
- Higher fees ($185 MRV fee + $79–$357 reciprocity fee)
- Expanded social media screening adding processing time in 2026

**Cost:** $264–$542 USD

### Decision Flowchart

```
Are you a Canadian citizen?
├── YES → Are you currently in the US on another valid status?
│   ├── YES → Do you want to stay in the US during processing?
│   │   ├── YES → Method B (I-129 with USCIS)
│   │   └── NO → Method A (Port of Entry) — faster and cheaper
│   └── NO → Method A (Port of Entry) — recommended
└── NO → Are you a Mexican citizen?
    ├── YES → Are you currently in the US on valid status?
    │   ├── YES → Method B (I-129) or Method C (Consular) if traveling
    │   └── NO → Method C (Consular Application) — required
    └── NO → TN visa is only for Canadian and Mexican citizens
```

## 1.5 Day-Of Walkthrough: Port of Entry Application

### Before You Arrive

1. **Eat breakfast.** You may be waiting 1–4 hours.
2. **Dress professionally.** Business casual minimum. First impressions matter.
3. **Arrive early.** Be at the port within the first hour of TN processing hours.
4. **Have documents organized.** Passport on top, employer letter next, then credentials.

### At the Port of Entry

1. **Approach the primary inspection booth.** Tell the officer: "I'm applying for TN status."
2. **You'll be directed to secondary inspection.** This is normal — all TN applications go to secondary.
3. **Wait to be called.** Could be 30 minutes to 3 hours depending on volume.
4. **When called, present your documents.** Hand over your passport and employer letter first.
5. **Answer questions clearly and concisely.** The officer will ask about your job, qualifications, and intent.
6. **Common questions include:**
   - "What will you be doing for this company?"
   - "How long do you plan to work in the US?"
   - "What is your educational background?"
   - "Do you plan to return to Canada?"
   - "Have you applied for TN status before?"
7. **Pay the $56 fee** when prompted.
8. **If approved:** You'll receive a stamped I-94 (or electronic I-94). Confirm the dates are correct before leaving.
9. **If denied:** Ask the officer to explain the reason. You can try again at a different port or on a different day — there is no formal bar on reapplying.

## 2.6 Credential Evaluation Guide

### When Do You Need a Credential Evaluation?

You need one if your degree was earned at an institution **outside** the United States, Canada, or Mexico. The evaluation confirms your foreign degree is equivalent to a US bachelor's degree (or higher) in the relevant field.

**You do NOT need an evaluation if:**
- Your degree is from a Canadian university (English or French)
- Your degree is from a US university
- Your degree is from a Mexican university (though having one can strengthen your case)

**You DO need an evaluation if:**
- Your degree is from a university in any other country
- Your degree is from a non-accredited institution
- Your degree title doesn't clearly indicate the field of study

### Recommended Evaluation Services

| Service | Type | Cost | Processing Time | Website |
|---------|------|------|-----------------|---------|
| **WES (World Education Services)** | Document-by-Document | $118 USD | 7 business days (after documents received) | wes.org |
| **WES** | Course-by-Course | $186 USD | 7 business days | wes.org |
| **ECE (Educational Credential Evaluators)** | General | $130 USD | 10 business days | ece.org |
| **ECE** | Course-by-Course | $195 USD | 10 business days | ece.org |
| **IQAS** (Alberta-based, for Canadian immigration) | Standard | $200 CAD | 12–16 weeks | alberta.ca |

> **Recommendation:** Use **WES Document-by-Document** for TN applications. It's the most widely recognized, fastest, and most affordable option. The Course-by-Course evaluation is only needed if the officer or USCIS specifically requires detailed course equivalency.

### WES Process Step-by-Step

1. **Create a WES account** at wes.org and select "WES Basic" evaluation
2. **Pay the fee** ($118 USD for Document-by-Document)
3. **Request your institution send documents directly to WES** — WES requires documents sent from the issuing institution, not from you
4. **Wait for WES to receive and verify documents** (1–4 weeks depending on your institution's speed)
5. **WES completes the evaluation** (7 business days after receiving all documents)
6. **Receive your evaluation report** by mail or electronically

**Total timeline: 3–7 weeks** from start to finish. Start this process EARLY.

## 2.7 Degree Verification Process

Even if you don't need a credential evaluation (because your degree is from Canada, the US, or Mexico), you should verify that your degree documentation is complete and presentable.

### Checklist for Degree Verification

- [ ] **Original diploma/degree certificate** — not a photocopy, not a scan. The original document with the university seal.
- [ ] **Official transcripts** — sealed in the university envelope if possible. Shows courses completed and grades earned.
- [ ] **Degree name matches TN category** — if your degree is in "Applied Computing" but you're applying as an "Engineer," you may need additional evidence showing the engineering content of your program.
- [ ] **Degree is at least a bachelor's level** — most TN categories require a minimum of a bachelor's degree. Diplomas and certificates generally don't qualify (exception: Scientific Technicians may qualify with a post-secondary diploma + 3 years experience).

### What If Your Degree Doesn't Perfectly Match?

This is common and not necessarily a dealbreaker. Strategies include:

1. **Supplement with transcripts** showing relevant coursework (e.g., engineering courses in a physics degree)
2. **Provide a credential evaluation** that explicitly states the equivalency
3. **Include professional certifications** that demonstrate expertise in the TN field
4. **Provide letters from professors or academic advisors** confirming the program's relevance
5. **Show years of professional experience** in the field (especially for Management Consultants)

## 3.2 Getting Your Social Security Number (SSN)

Your SSN is the key to everything in America — banking, credit, employment, taxes, housing. Get this done as soon as possible.

### When to Apply

**Wait at least 10 days after your TN admission** before visiting an SSA office. This allows time for your immigration status to be entered into the DHS database that SSA checks. Applying too early results in delays or rejection.

### What to Bring to the SSA Office

1. **Completed Form SS-5** (Application for a Social Security Card) — download from ssa.gov/forms/ss-5.pdf or fill it out at the office
2. **Your original passport** (Canadian or Mexican)
3. **Your original I-94** (print from i94.cbp.dhs.gov — the electronic version is acceptable)
4. **Your I-797 approval notice** (if you applied via I-129)
5. **Your birth certificate** (if available — passport can substitute as proof of age)

> **Important:** SSA requires ORIGINAL documents or certified copies from the issuing agency. Photocopies and notarized copies are NOT accepted.

### Step-by-Step Process

1. **Find your nearest SSA office** at ssa.gov/locator
2. **Check if appointments are required** — some offices are walk-in only, others require appointments. Call ahead.
3. **Arrive early** — SSA offices can have long wait times, especially in major cities
4. **Submit Form SS-5 and your documents** — the clerk will verify your identity and immigration status
5. **Receive a receipt** — your SSN card will be mailed to your US address within **2–4 weeks**
6. **Your SSN is assigned immediately** — even before the card arrives, you can call SSA after 1 week to get your number verbally (useful for starting employment paperwork)

### Tips

- Your employer can start your I-9 employment verification with your passport and I-94 — you don't need the SSN card to begin working
- If you don't receive your card within 4 weeks, call SSA at 1-800-772-1213
- Keep your SSN card in a safe place — do NOT carry it in your wallet
- You'll use your SSN for: bank accounts, credit applications, tax filing, driver's license, health insurance enrollment

## 3.4 Building US Credit from Scratch

Your Canadian credit history does NOT transfer to the United States. Equifax, TransUnion, and Experian maintain completely separate databases for Canadian and American consumers. You're starting from zero.

Here's the fastest path to building US credit:

### Strategy 1: American Express Global Transfer (Fastest)

If you have a Canadian Amex card in good standing for at least 3 months:

1. **Call Amex Global Transfer** at 1-800-528-4800 (US) and tell them you've moved to the US
2. **Apply for a US Amex card** — Amex uses your Canadian cardholder history to approve you, bypassing the US credit check
3. **Best first card:** Amex Blue Cash Everyday (no annual fee) or Amex Gold (if you want rewards)
4. **Your US Amex card reports to US credit bureaus** — this starts building your US credit file immediately

> **Pro Tip:** This is the single fastest way to establish US credit. If you don't have a Canadian Amex, get one NOW (even a no-fee card) and wait 3 months before your move.

### Strategy 2: Nova Credit (If You Don't Have Amex)

Nova Credit is a service that translates your Canadian credit history for US lenders:

1. Go to **novacredit.com**
2. Select a partner credit card (options include Amex, HSBC, and others)
3. Nova Credit pulls your Canadian credit report and presents it to the US lender
4. If approved, the card reports to US credit bureaus

### Strategy 3: Secured Credit Card (Backup Plan)

If neither Amex Global Transfer nor Nova Credit works:

1. **Open a secured credit card** — you deposit $200–$500 as collateral, and that becomes your credit limit
2. **Best options:** Discover it® Secured, Capital One Platinum Secured
3. **Use it for small purchases** and pay the full balance every month
4. **After 6–12 months**, you'll have enough credit history to qualify for regular cards

### Credit Building Timeline

| Month | Action | Expected Credit Score |
|-------|--------|---------------------|
| 0 | Arrive in US — no credit file exists | N/A |
| 1 | Open Amex via Global Transfer or secured card | Score generated after ~1 month |
| 2–3 | Use card responsibly, pay in full monthly | 650–680 |
| 4–6 | Apply for Chase Freedom or similar | 680–720 |
| 6–12 | Continue responsible use, keep utilization under 30% | 720–750 |

### The Chase 5/24 Rule

Chase will deny most credit card applications if you've opened 5 or more credit cards (across all banks) in the past 24 months. Since you're starting fresh, plan your card applications strategically:
- **Months 1–3:** Amex Global Transfer card only
- **Months 4–6:** Apply for your first Chase card (Freedom Flex or Sapphire Preferred)
- **Stay under 5 new cards in 24 months** to keep Chase options open

## 3.6 Health Insurance Options

### Employer-Sponsored Insurance (Most Common)

Most TN workers get health insurance through their employer. Key things to know:

- **Enrollment window:** Usually within 30 days of your start date (this is your "qualifying life event")
- **Don't miss this window** — if you do, you'll have to wait until open enrollment (typically November–December)
- **Types of plans:** HMO (cheaper, restricted network), PPO (more expensive, more flexibility), HDHP (high deductible, pairs with HSA)
- **Typical costs:** $100–$400/month for individual coverage (employer pays the rest); $300–$1,200/month for family
- **Coverage starts:** Usually the 1st of the month following your start date, or after a 30–90 day waiting period

### ACA Marketplace (If Employer Doesn't Offer Insurance)

If your employer doesn't offer health insurance, or if you're in a waiting period:

- **Healthcare.gov** — the federal marketplace for health insurance
- **Open enrollment:** November 1 – January 15 annually
- **Special enrollment:** You qualify for a 60-day special enrollment period when you first move to the US
- **Costs:** $200–$800/month depending on plan level and location
- **Subsidies:** Available based on income — TN workers with moderate salaries may qualify

### Short-Term Coverage (Bridge Insurance)

For the gap between arriving and employer coverage starting:

- **Travel insurance from Canada** — extend your existing travel medical insurance (e.g., Manulife CoverMe, Blue Cross)
- **Short-term US health plans** — available through companies like United Healthcare or Cigna
- **Cost:** $50–$200/month for basic coverage
- **Duration:** 1–3 months

> **Warning:** The US has NO universal healthcare. A single ER visit without insurance can cost $5,000–$50,000+. Do NOT go without coverage, even for a few weeks.

## 3.7 Finding Housing Without US Credit

This is one of the biggest challenges for new TN arrivals. Most landlords run credit checks, and you won't have a US credit score initially.

### Strategies That Work

1. **Offer to pay several months upfront** — 2–3 months' rent in advance often convinces landlords to skip the credit check
2. **Provide a larger security deposit** — offer 2x the normal deposit
3. **Show proof of income** — your offer letter with salary, first pay stubs, or bank statements showing savings
4. **Get a letter from your employer** — on company letterhead, confirming your position, salary, and employment duration
5. **Use your Canadian credit report** — some landlords will accept a Canadian Equifax or TransUnion report as evidence of creditworthiness
6. **Corporate housing** — some employers offer relocation assistance or corporate housing for the first few months
7. **Furnished apartments / extended-stay hotels** — more expensive but no credit check required. Good for the first 1–2 months while you build credit.
8. **Sublets** — find someone subletting their apartment on Craigslist, Facebook groups, or Reddit. Often no formal credit check.
9. **Apartment complexes that accept international applicants** — larger complexes (especially near tech hubs) are more experienced with international workers

### What Landlords Want to See

- Proof of income (offer letter or pay stubs showing income ≥ 3x monthly rent)
- Valid ID (passport)
- Proof of immigration status (I-94, I-797)
- References (previous landlord, employer)
- Bank statements showing sufficient savings

## 3.9 Canadian Tax Obligations When You Leave

This is where most Canadians get blindsided. Moving to the US does NOT end your Canadian tax obligations — at least not immediately.

### The Departure Tax (Deemed Disposition)

When you become a non-resident of Canada for tax purposes, the CRA treats you as having **sold all your non-exempt assets** at fair market value on the day you leave. This is called a "deemed disposition."

**Assets SUBJECT to deemed disposition (you owe tax on gains):**
- Non-registered investment accounts (taxable brokerage accounts)
- Shares of private Canadian corporations (CCPCs)
- Non-Canadian real estate
- Most personal property above certain thresholds

**Assets EXEMPT from deemed disposition:**
- Canadian real estate (land and buildings)
- RRSP, RRIF, LIRA, RPP (protected by US-Canada Tax Treaty)
- CPP and OAS entitlements

**What this means:** If you have a non-registered investment account with $50,000 in unrealized gains, the CRA will tax you on that $50,000 gain even though you didn't actually sell anything.

### Form NR73: Determining Your Residency Status

If your ties to Canada are ambiguous (e.g., you still own property, your spouse stays behind temporarily), file **Form NR73** with the CRA to get a formal determination of your residency status.

**Primary ties that keep you a Canadian tax resident:**
- A home available for your use in Canada
- A spouse or common-law partner who remains in Canada
- Dependents who remain in Canada

**Secondary ties (matter collectively):**
- Canadian bank accounts, credit cards, investments
- Canadian driver's license, health card
- Social memberships, professional affiliations
- Canadian mailing address

> **Critical:** If your family stays behind in Canada, you are likely still a Canadian tax resident — meaning the CRA taxes your worldwide income, including your US salary.

### What to Do with Your RRSP

- **Do NOT withdraw it** — the RRSP is protected by the US-Canada Tax Treaty at the federal level
- **You cannot contribute** after becoming a non-resident
- **Consider "crystallization"** before you leave: sell all holdings inside the RRSP and immediately repurchase them. This resets the cost basis for US tax purposes, reducing future US tax on withdrawals
- **14 US states do NOT recognize the treaty** and will tax RRSP income annually: Alabama, Arkansas, California, Connecticut, Georgia, Hawaii, Kansas, Kentucky, Maryland, Mississippi, Montana, New Jersey, North Dakota, Pennsylvania

### What to Do with Your TFSA

**Close it before you move.** This is the strongest recommendation in this entire guide.

- The US does NOT recognize the TFSA's tax-free status
- The IRS treats it as a taxable foreign grantor trust
- All income inside the TFSA is taxable in the US annually
- If it holds Canadian mutual funds or ETFs, those are classified as PFICs (Passive Foreign Investment Companies) — the reporting is complex and the tax treatment is punitive
- You cannot contribute to a TFSA as a non-resident (1% per month penalty on excess contributions)

### Canadian Tax Filing Requirements After You Leave

- **File a T1 departure return** for the year you leave (includes departure date and deemed disposition calculations)
- **If you keep Canadian rental property:** File a Section 216 return annually and arrange for 25% withholding on gross rent
- **If you receive CPP/OAS:** These continue but are subject to withholding at source
- **Repay any outstanding HBP or LLP balances** before departure — otherwise the full balance is added to your income

## 3.10 US Tax Obligations — What the IRS Needs from You

### The Substantial Presence Test (SPT)

The IRS determines your tax residency using the Substantial Presence Test. You're a US tax resident if you're physically present for:
- At least **31 days** in the current year, AND
- At least **183 days** over a 3-year weighted period (current year × 1 + prior year × ⅓ + two years ago × ⅙)

Once you meet this test, the IRS taxes your **worldwide income**.

### Key US Tax Forms

| Form | What It Is | Who Files It | Deadline |
|------|-----------|-------------|----------|
| **Form 1040** | US Individual Income Tax Return | You | April 15 (or October 15 with extension) |
| **FBAR (FinCEN 114)** | Foreign Bank Account Report — required if aggregate foreign accounts exceed $10,000 at any point during the year | You | April 15 (automatic extension to October 15) |
| **Form 8938 (FATCA)** | Statement of Specified Foreign Financial Assets — required if foreign assets exceed $50,000 (single) at year-end | You | Filed with Form 1040 |
| **Form 8833** | Treaty-Based Return Position — used to claim US-Canada Tax Treaty benefits (e.g., RRSP deferral) | You | Filed with Form 1040 |
| **Form 8621** | PFIC reporting — required for each Canadian mutual fund or ETF you own | You | Filed with Form 1040 |
| **Form W-4** | Employee's Withholding Certificate — tells your employer how much tax to withhold | You (give to employer) | When you start your job |

### FBAR: The Form Most Canadians Miss

If the **total combined value** of all your foreign (Canadian) financial accounts exceeds **$10,000 at any point during the year**, you must file an FBAR. This includes:
- Canadian bank accounts (chequing, savings)
- Canadian investment accounts
- RRSP, TFSA, LIRA, RRIF
- Any other Canadian financial account

**Penalties for not filing:**
- Non-willful: up to $10,000 per violation
- Willful: up to $100,000 per violation or 50% of account balance

> **This is not optional.** The IRS actively enforces FBAR compliance. File it every year, even if you owe no tax.

### Year One: The Dual-Status Return

In the year you move, you'll likely file a **dual-status return** — part of the year as a non-resident alien (before establishing US residency) and part as a resident alien (after). This is the most complex return you'll ever file. **Hire a cross-border tax professional for your first year.** The cost ($500–$2,000) is worth it to avoid five-figure penalties.

# Part 4: Renewal Preparation Guide

TN status is granted for up to 3 years at a time and can be renewed **indefinitely** — there is no maximum number of renewals. But each renewal requires preparation, and the process has pitfalls that catch people off guard.

## 4.1 When to Start Preparing

**Start 6 months before your I-94 expiry date.** Here's why:

| Months Before Expiry | Action |
|----------------------|--------|
| **6 months** | Begin gathering updated documents. Confirm your employer will support renewal. |
| **4–5 months** | Draft updated employer support letter. Order new credential evaluation if needed. |
| **3–4 months** | If using I-129: employer should file the petition now (standard processing takes 3–7 months) |
| **2–3 months** | If using POE: finalize all documents and plan your border trip |
| **1 month** | Final review of all documents. Practice interview questions. Book travel if needed. |
| **2 weeks** | Confirm port of entry hours. Do a final document check. |

> **Why 6 months?** If you're using the I-129 method, standard processing can take 3–7 months. Filing 6 months early gives you a comfortable buffer. Even for POE renewals, starting early means you have time to fix any document issues.

---

## 4.2 Three Renewal Methods Compared

### Method 1: Port of Entry Renewal (Canadians Only)

**How it works:** You leave the US (or go to a Canadian pre-clearance airport), present updated documents to a CBP officer, and get a new I-94 on the spot.

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Eligible** | Canadian citizens only |
| **Cost** | $56 ($50 application + $6 I-94) |
| **Processing time** | Same day (1–4 hours) |
| **Risk** | Denial means you're outside the US |
| **Best for** | People comfortable with border interviews who want speed and low cost |

**Documents needed:**
- Valid Canadian passport
- Updated employer support letter (confirming continued employment, current duties, salary, and requested extension period)
- Original degree/diploma and transcripts
- Professional license (if applicable)
- Previous I-94 records
- Previous I-797 approval notices (if any)
- Evidence of temporary intent

### Method 2: Form I-129 Extension (Both Nationalities)

**How it works:** Your employer files Form I-129 with USCIS requesting an extension of your TN status. You stay in the US during processing.

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Eligible** | Canadian and Mexican citizens |
| **Cost** | $810–$4,420+ (base fee + Asylum Program Fee + optional premium processing) |
| **Processing time** | 3–7 months standard; 15 business days with premium processing ($2,965) |
| **Risk** | Low — you can keep working for up to 240 days past I-94 expiry while pending |
| **Best for** | People who don't want to leave the US, or who want the security of the 240-day rule |

**Documents needed:**
- Everything in the POE list above, PLUS:
- Completed Form I-129 with TN supplement
- Filing fees
- Copy of current I-94
- Copy of passport biographical page

### Method 3: Consular Renewal (Mexican Citizens)

**How it works:** You schedule a new DS-160 appointment at a US consulate in Mexico, attend the interview, and receive a new visa stamp.

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Eligible** | Mexican citizens (required if visa stamp has expired) |
| **Cost** | $264–$542 (MRV fee + reciprocity fee) |
| **Processing time** | 1–4 weeks for interview scheduling + 5–10 days for visa stamp |
| **Risk** | Must leave the US for the interview |
| **Best for** | Mexican citizens whose visa stamp has expired |

---

## 4.3 What Changes from Your First Application

Renewals are generally easier than first-time applications, but there are key differences:

| Aspect | First Application | Renewal |
|--------|------------------|---------|
| **Scrutiny level** | Higher — officer is evaluating you for the first time | Lower — you have a track record of compliance |
| **Documents** | Must prove everything from scratch | Can reference prior approvals |
| **Employer letter** | Must be extremely detailed | Still needs to be detailed, but can reference continuity |
| **Questions asked** | Broad — qualifications, intent, job duties | Focused — "has anything changed?" |
| **Approval rate** | ~95% | Higher than first-time (assuming no issues) |
| **New requirement** | N/A | Must show you've been working in the approved role (pay stubs, tax returns) |

### What to Emphasize in Your Renewal

- **Continuity:** Same employer, same role, same duties
- **Compliance:** You've been working legally, filing taxes, maintaining status
- **Temporary intent:** You still plan to return to Canada/Mexico when your employment ends
- **Updated salary:** If your salary has increased, update the employer letter

---

## 4.4 Common Renewal Pitfalls

### Pitfall 1: Waiting Too Long to File

If your I-94 expires before your I-129 extension is filed, you lose the 240-day work authorization protection. File early — at least 3–4 months before expiry for I-129, or plan your POE trip well in advance.

### Pitfall 2: Traveling While I-129 Is Pending

**This is the #1 most costly mistake in TN renewals.** If you leave the US while your I-129 extension is pending, USCIS generally treats the petition as **abandoned**. You lose:
- The pending petition
- The 240-day work authorization
- Your current TN status

To re-enter, you'd need to apply fresh at a POE (Canadians) or consulate (Mexicans).

> **Rule:** Do NOT travel internationally while an I-129 extension is pending unless you've consulted an immigration attorney about your specific situation.

### Pitfall 3: Job Duties That Have Changed

If your actual job duties have drifted significantly from what's described in your original employer letter, this can cause problems at renewal. The officer may determine your current role no longer qualifies under your TN category.

**Prevention:** Review your employer letter annually. If your duties have changed, update the letter to accurately reflect your current role while ensuring it still maps to your USMCA profession.

### Pitfall 4: Expired Passport

Your TN status cannot extend beyond your passport expiry date. If your passport expires before your desired 3-year TN extension, you'll only get TN status until the passport expiry.

**Prevention:** Renew your passport before applying for TN renewal. Canadian passport renewal takes 10–20 business days.

### Pitfall 5: Gaps in Employment

If you were unemployed for any period during your TN status, this raises questions. TN status is tied to specific employment — if you weren't working, you may have been out of status.

**Prevention:** If you lose your job, you have a 60-day grace period to find new TN-eligible employment, change status, or depart the US.

---

## 4.5 What If Your Employer Changed?

Changing employers on TN status requires a **new TN approval** — you cannot simply transfer your existing TN to a new employer.

### Options for Changing Employers

1. **File a new I-129** with the new employer as petitioner (you can continue working for your current employer while it processes, but cannot start with the new employer until approved)
2. **Exit the US and re-enter at a POE** with documentation from your new employer (Canadians only — fastest method)
3. **Apply for a new TN visa at a consulate** with new employer documentation (Mexicans)

### Concurrent TN Employment

You CAN work for multiple employers simultaneously on TN status, but each employer needs its own separate TN approval. You'd file separate I-129 petitions or make separate POE applications for each employer.

---

## 4.6 What If Your Duties Changed?

If your job title or duties have changed significantly, you may need to file an **amended I-129 petition** with USCIS, even if you're staying with the same employer.

**When an amendment is needed:**
- Your job title changed to a different USMCA profession category
- Your core duties shifted substantially (e.g., from software engineering to project management)
- Your work location changed to a different state

**When an amendment is NOT needed:**
- Minor duty changes within the same profession
- Salary increase
- Promotion within the same professional category
- Same duties at a different office of the same employer (in most cases)

---

## 4.7 The 240-Day Automatic Extension Rule

This is your safety net for I-129 renewals. Here's how it works:

**If your employer files Form I-129 to extend your TN status BEFORE your current I-94 expires:**
- You can continue working for that same employer for up to **240 days** past your I-94 expiry date while USCIS processes the extension
- Your employer should note this on your I-9 form and keep copies of the I-129 receipt notice

**Critical conditions:**
- The petition must be filed **before** your I-94 expires — even one day late and the protection doesn't apply
- You can only work for the **same employer** who filed the petition
- If USCIS **denies** the petition, your work authorization ends immediately upon receiving the denial
- If you **leave the US** while the petition is pending, the protection is lost

> **Best practice:** File the I-129 extension at least 3–4 months before your I-94 expires. This gives USCIS time to process within the 240-day window even with standard processing.

| 6+ months before move | Start cross-border tax planning | Missed optimization opportunities (RRSP crystallization, TFSA closure) |
| 4 weeks before application | Begin document preparation | Rushed application, missing documents |
| Before departure | Close TFSA | IRS treats as taxable foreign trust; PFIC reporting nightmare |
| Before departure | Repay HBP/LLP balances | Full balance added to income on departure T1 |
| Before departure | File NR73 (if needed) | Ambiguous residency status; potential double taxation |

### After Your Move

| Deadline | Action | Consequence of Missing |
|----------|--------|----------------------|
| 10+ days after entry | Apply for SSN | Delays in banking, employment paperwork |
| 30 days after start date | Enroll in employer health insurance | Must wait until next open enrollment |
| April 15 | File US tax return (Form 1040) | Penalties and interest |
| April 15 | File FBAR (FinCEN 114) — auto-extends to Oct 15 | Penalties up to $10,000+ per violation |
| April 30 | File Canadian T1 departure return (for year of move) | Late filing penalties |
| 6 months before TN expiry | Begin renewal preparation | Risk of status gap or rushed filing |
| Before TN expiry | File I-129 extension (if using that method) | Loss of 240-day work authorization protection |

### Ongoing Annual Obligations

| When | What | Where |
|------|------|-------|
| January–April | File US Form 1040 | IRS |
| January–April | File FBAR if foreign accounts > $10,000 | FinCEN (fincen.gov/bsa-e-filing) |
| January–April | File Form 8938 if foreign assets > $50,000 | With Form 1040 |
| January–April | File Canadian non-resident return (if Canadian-source income) | CRA |
| Annually | Renew state driver's license (if tied to I-94 expiry) | State DMV |
| Every 3 years | Renew TN status | POE, USCIS, or Consulate |

## 5.4 Glossary of Immigration Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **CBP** | Customs and Border Protection — the agency that processes you at ports of entry |
| **CRA** | Canada Revenue Agency — Canada's tax authority |
| **Deemed Disposition** | CRA treats you as having sold assets at fair market value when you leave Canada |
| **DS-160** | Online Nonimmigrant Visa Application — required for Mexican citizens applying at a consulate |
| **Dual-Status Return** | A US tax return covering part of the year as non-resident and part as resident |
| **EAD** | Employment Authorization Document — NOT needed for TN workers (your I-94 is your work authorization) |
| **FBAR** | Foreign Bank Account Report (FinCEN 114) — required if foreign accounts exceed $10,000 |
| **FATCA** | Foreign Account Tax Compliance Act — requires reporting foreign assets on Form 8938 |
| **Form I-94** | Arrival/Departure Record — your proof of legal admission and authorized stay period |
| **Form I-129** | Petition for a Nonimmigrant Worker — filed by your employer to request or extend TN status |
| **Form I-539** | Application to Extend/Change Nonimmigrant Status — used by TD dependents |
| **Form I-797** | USCIS approval notice — proof that your I-129 petition was approved |
| **Form I-907** | Request for Premium Processing — guarantees 15-business-day adjudication |
| **I-9** | Employment Eligibility Verification — your employer completes this to verify your work authorization |
| **ITIN** | Individual Taxpayer Identification Number — used for tax filing if you don't have an SSN |
| **LPR** | Lawful Permanent Resident (green card holder) — NOT the same as TN status |
| **MRV Fee** | Machine Readable Visa fee — the $185 consular application fee for Mexican citizens |
| **NACES** | National Association of Credential Evaluation Services — accredits evaluation agencies |
| **NR73** | CRA form to determine your Canadian residency status when leaving Canada |
| **PFIC** | Passive Foreign Investment Company — how the IRS classifies Canadian mutual funds and ETFs |
| **POE** | Port of Entry — a border crossing or pre-clearance airport where you apply for TN status |
| **Premium Processing** | USCIS service guaranteeing action on your petition within 15 business days ($2,965) |
| **SPT** | Substantial Presence Test — how the IRS determines if you're a US tax resident |
| **SSN** | Social Security Number — your US identification number for taxes, banking, and credit |
| **TD Status** | Trade Dependent — the status given to spouses and children of TN visa holders |
| **TN Status** | Trade National — the nonimmigrant classification for USMCA professionals |
| **USCIS** | US Citizenship and Immigration Services — processes I-129 petitions and other immigration benefits |
| **USMCA** | United States-Mexico-Canada Agreement — the trade agreement that governs TN visa eligibility (replaced NAFTA) |
| **WES** | World Education Services — the most widely used credential evaluation service |
| **240-Day Rule** | Allows continued employment for up to 240 days past I-94 expiry if I-129 extension was timely filed |
